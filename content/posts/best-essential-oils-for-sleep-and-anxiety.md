Scent is closely linked to the parts of the brain that handle emotion and memory. That's why a familiar smell can relax you almost instantly. Essential oils won't cure insomnia or anxiety, but they can be a pleasant cue that it's time to slow down.

## Our favourite calming oils

**Lavender.** This is the most studied relaxing oil. Several small trials suggest that inhaling lavender may improve how people rate their sleep quality and reduce mild anxiety.

**Bergamot.** A citrus oil that's uplifting yet calming. Note that it's phototoxic on skin, so don't apply it before going in the sun.

**Roman chamomile.** It has a sweet, apple-like scent, and it's lovely blended with lavender.

**Cedarwood.** Woody and grounding, it's a favourite in "sleep" diffuser blends.

**Clary sage.** Herbal and musky, it's often used for relaxation.

**Frankincense.** Resinous and warm, it's often used during meditation.

## How to use them safely

- **Diffuse** 3 to 5 drops in a diffuser for 30 to 60 minutes before bed, in a ventilated room.
- **Put 1 to 2 drops on a pillow corner or a tissue**, not directly where your face rests.
- **Dilute before skin use.** For adults, use about 2 to 3 drops per teaspoon of carrier oil (sweet almond, jojoba), which is roughly 1 to 2%.
- **Never swallow essential oils** unless a qualified professional directs you to.

## Who should take extra care

- **Babies, young children and pregnant women.** Many oils aren't suitable, so check first.
- **Pets.** Cats in particular are sensitive to many oils, including tea tree and citrus.
- **Asthma.** Strong scents can trigger symptoms.

## Pair it with tea

Our favourite bedtime routine is a diffuser of lavender and cedarwood, plus a cup of [chamomile](/chamomile-tea-benefits) or [passionflower](/passionflower-tea-wonderful-benefits) tea.

If anxiety or poor sleep is affecting your daily life, talk to your GP. Effective help is available.
