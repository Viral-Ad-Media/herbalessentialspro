Essential oils are highly concentrated plant extracts, usually made by steam-distilling leaves, flowers, bark or peel. A single drop of peppermint oil can contain the aromatic compounds of dozens of tea bags. That strength is exactly why safety comes first.

## Five rules before you start

1. **Dilute for skin.** Use a carrier oil (jojoba, sweet almond or fractionated coconut). About 1 to 2% is plenty for adults, which is 6 to 12 drops per 30 ml.
2. **Don't swallow them.** Internal use belongs with trained clinicians only.
3. **Patch test.** Apply a diluted drop inside your elbow and wait 24 hours.
4. **Mind the vulnerable.** Babies, children, pregnancy, pets, and people with asthma or epilepsy need extra caution.
5. **Store them safely.** Use dark glass bottles with child-resistant caps, kept away from heat and light.

## A simple starter kit

| Oil | Smells like | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Lavender | Floral, soft | Relaxation and bedtime |
| Sweet orange | Bright citrus | An uplifting room scent |
| Peppermint | Cool, sharp | Alertness (keep away from young children) |
| Tea tree | Medicinal, fresh | Diluted for skin blemishes |
| Eucalyptus | Camphor, clear | A steamy shower in cold season |

## Ways to use them

- **Diffuser:** 3 to 5 drops in water, run for short sessions.
- **Bath:** mix 3 to 5 drops into a tablespoon of carrier oil or unscented bath gel first. Oil and water don't mix.
- **Roller bottle:** pre-dilute a blend for wrists and temples.
- **Steam:** 1 to 2 drops in a bowl of hot water, with your eyes closed.

## Buying tips

Look for the **Latin name**, the **country of origin** and a **dark glass bottle**. Be wary of very cheap "lavender oil", which is often synthetic or cut with other oils.
