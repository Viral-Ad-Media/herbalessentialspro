Hibiscus tea is made from the dried calyces of *Hibiscus sabdariffa*, also called roselle. It's known as *agua de jamaica* in Mexico, *bissap* in West Africa and *karkadé* in Egypt.

## What it tastes like

Tart and fruity, a lot like cranberry. It's naturally caffeine-free and turns a striking deep red.

## Potential benefits

**Blood pressure.** Several clinical trials suggest that drinking hibiscus tea daily may modestly lower blood pressure in people with mild hypertension. It's not a replacement for medication, but it's one of the better-studied herbal teas.

**Antioxidants.** The red colour comes from anthocyanins, the same family of antioxidants found in berries.

**A sugar-free swap.** Iced hibiscus is a great replacement for fizzy drinks.

## How to brew it

**Hot:** steep 1 to 2 teaspoons of dried hibiscus in boiling water for 5 minutes.

**Iced (agua de jamaica):** simmer ½ cup of dried flowers in 4 cups of water for 10 minutes, then strain and chill. Sweeten lightly, and add lime, mint or a cinnamon stick.

## Who should be careful

- If you take **blood pressure medication**, hibiscus may add to its effect. Monitor your readings and tell your doctor.
- It may interact with some **diabetes medicines** and with **paracetamol (acetaminophen)**.
- **Avoid it during pregnancy.**
