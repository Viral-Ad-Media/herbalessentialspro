Dried herbs don't spoil the way fresh food does. They slowly fade instead. Aromatic oils evaporate, colours dull and flavour drains away. Good storage can keep them vibrant for a year or more.

## The four enemies

1. **Light** breaks down colour and aromatic compounds. Clear jars on a sunny shelf look lovely but age herbs fast.
2. **Heat** speeds up the loss of volatile oils. Keep herbs away from the hob, oven and kettle.
3. **Air** oxidises herbs over time. The less headspace in the jar, the better.
4. **Moisture** invites mould. Never store herbs near a steaming kettle, or in the fridge where condensation forms.

## The best containers

- **Amber or opaque glass jars** with tight lids.
- **Tins** with snug lids, which are great for tea blends.
- **Resealable kraft pouches** with a foil lining. Press the air out each time.

Avoid paper bags (they let air in) and thin plastic (it can absorb aromas).

## Smart habits

- **Label everything** with the herb and the date.
- **Buy whole when you can.** Whole leaves and flowers keep their flavour longer than powders.
- **Buy little and often.** A 50 g pouch you'll finish in two months beats a 500 g bag that fades.
- **Do the sniff test.** If you crush a pinch and there's almost no aroma, it's time to replace it.

## How long do they last?

| Type | Best within |
|---|---|
| Leaves and flowers (mint, chamomile, nettle) | 6 to 12 months |
| Seeds (fennel, anise) | 1 to 2 years |
| Roots and barks (ginger, cinnamon, dandelion) | 1 to 3 years |
| Powders | 3 to 6 months |

*Storing your own garden herbs? Make sure they're completely crisp-dry before jarring. Leaves should crumble, not bend.*
