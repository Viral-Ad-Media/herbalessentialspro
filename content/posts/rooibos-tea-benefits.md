Rooibos ("red bush") comes from *Aspalathus linearis*, a shrub that grows only in the Cederberg region of South Africa. The leaves are fermented, which turns them a deep red and gives the tea its naturally sweet, honeyed flavour.

## Why people love rooibos

**No caffeine.** Rooibos is a good choice for evenings, pregnancy (in moderation) and anyone sensitive to caffeine.

**Low in tannins.** Compared with black tea, it's less bitter and less likely to interfere with iron absorption.

**Antioxidants.** Rooibos contains aspalathin and nothofagin, two antioxidants that are rare in other plants.

**It tastes like a treat.** It takes milk well, so it's a natural swap for an afternoon builder's tea.

## How to brew it

Rooibos is forgiving. Use 1 tea bag or 1 teaspoon of loose leaves per cup, pour on boiling water and steep for 5 to 10 minutes. It won't turn bitter if you forget about it. Try it with milk and a pinch of cinnamon, or iced with orange slices.

**Green rooibos** is unfermented. It's lighter and grassier, and slightly higher in antioxidants.

## Who should be careful

Rooibos is very well tolerated. If you have a **hormone-sensitive condition** or **liver disease**, or you take **chemotherapy** drugs, check with your doctor before drinking large amounts.
