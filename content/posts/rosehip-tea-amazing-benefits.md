When wild roses (*Rosa canina*, the dog rose) finish flowering, they leave behind small orange-red fruits called hips. People have foraged them for centuries. During the Second World War, rosehip syrup was given to British children as a vitamin C source.

## Why brew rosehips

**Vitamin C.** Fresh rosehips are very rich in vitamin C. Drying and brewing lose some of it, but the tea still contributes.

**Antioxidants.** Rosehips contain carotenoids, flavonoids and polyphenols.

**Joint comfort.** Rosehip powder has been studied for osteoarthritis, with some promising results. The tea is a milder, food-level way to enjoy it.

**Flavour.** It's tangy and slightly sweet, and blends beautifully with hibiscus.

![Rosehip tea with fresh berries](/uploads/2022/08/depositphotos_85819362-stock-photo-tea-rosehip-vitamin-drink.webp)

## How to brew it

Lightly crush 1 to 2 teaspoons of dried rosehips, steep them in boiling water for 10 to 15 minutes, then strain. If you use fresh hips, remove the hairy seeds first, as they can irritate the throat.

## Who should be careful

Rosehip is generally safe as a tea. Talk to your doctor if you have **kidney stones**, **iron overload (haemochromatosis)** or take **blood thinners**.
