Common sage (*Salvia officinalis*) has soft, grey-green leaves and an earthy, slightly peppery taste. Its Latin name comes from *salvere*, meaning "to be saved", which tells you how highly it was once regarded.

## What sage tea may help with

**Sore throats.** A warm sage gargle or tea is a traditional remedy for a scratchy throat, thanks to the herb's astringent and antimicrobial compounds.

**Hot flushes.** Sage has a long folk use for menopausal sweating, and a few small studies support it.

**Oral health.** Sage is a common ingredient in natural mouthwashes.

**Antioxidants.** Like many herbs, sage is rich in polyphenols such as rosmarinic acid.

## How to brew it

Steep 1 teaspoon of dried sage, or 5 to 6 fresh leaves, in hot water for 5 minutes. A little honey and lemon soften its flavour.

## Why moderation matters

Common sage contains **thujone**, a compound that can be harmful in large amounts. One or two cups a day for a short period is generally considered fine for healthy adults. Avoid:

- drinking large amounts every day for weeks,
- sage **essential oil** taken internally,
- sage tea during **pregnancy or breastfeeding** (it may reduce milk supply),
- sage if you have a **seizure disorder**.
