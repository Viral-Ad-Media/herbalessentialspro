For most people, a few cups of tea a day are perfectly safe. Herbs are still active plants, though, and some deserve respect, especially in large amounts, during pregnancy, or alongside medication.

## Caffeine (black, green, white and oolong tea, and matcha)
Too much can cause jitters, a racing heart, poor sleep and headaches. Most adults are fine with up to about 400 mg a day from all sources, which is roughly 8 cups of black tea. The limit is lower in pregnancy.

## Iron absorption
Tannins in black and green tea can reduce iron absorption from plant foods. If you're low in iron, drink tea **between** meals rather than with them.

## Herb-specific cautions

| Herb | Watch out for |
|---|---|
| Peppermint | Can worsen heartburn and reflux |
| Chamomile, echinacea | Allergy if you react to ragweed or daisies |
| Hibiscus | Lowers blood pressure; avoid in pregnancy |
| Sage | Thujone; avoid large daily amounts |
| Liquorice root | Raises blood pressure; lowers potassium |
| Senna ("detox" teas) | Laxative; not for regular use |
| St John's wort | Interacts with many medicines, including the contraceptive pill |

## Medication interactions
Blood thinners, blood pressure and diabetes medicines, sedatives and antidepressants are the most common sources of problems. If you take regular medication, show your pharmacist the teas you drink.

## Temperature
Let very hot drinks cool for a few minutes. Regularly drinking scalding liquids is linked to damage in the oesophagus.
