Your immune system relies on sleep, nutrition, movement and managing stress. Tea can't replace any of those, but a warm cup can support your habits, keep you hydrated and bring a few useful plant compounds along with it.

## Our top picks

**Echinacea.** It's the classic choice at the first sign of a cold. The evidence is mixed but encouraging for short-term use. [Read our echinacea guide](/echinacea-tea-benefits).

**Ginger.** It's warming, soothing for sore throats and good for queasiness. [Read more about ginger](/ginger-tea-health-benefits).

**Elderberry.** It's rich in anthocyanins, and some small studies link it to shorter colds and flu.

**Rosehip.** It's a fruity source of vitamin C and antioxidants. [Read about rosehip](/rosehip-tea-amazing-benefits).

**Green tea.** It contains catechins such as EGCG, along with a little caffeine.

**Lemongrass, turmeric and lemon.** These make a bright, aromatic blend that's lovely when you're feeling run down.

## A simple cold-season blend

Simmer for 10 minutes:

- 3 slices of fresh ginger
- ½ teaspoon of turmeric
- 1 teaspoon of dried echinacea or elderberry
- 2 cups of water

Strain, then add lemon and honey. Honey isn't suitable for children under one.

## Keep it in perspective

See a doctor if you have a high fever, difficulty breathing, or symptoms that last more than 10 days. Check herb and medicine interactions if you take regular prescriptions.
